# Presentation quality rubric

Use this rubric to judge whether a deck teaches, not merely whether it is
complete and accurate. Score concrete evidence in the deck and its artifacts.
Do not award points for stated intent that the learner never sees.

For a new or materially revised deck, aim for an average score of at least 3.0
with no dimension scored 1. A lower score is a review finding, not permission to
inflate the deck or add filler. Fix the teaching problem that caused it.

For an existing deck audit, report the scores and evidence before proposing
changes. Do not edit the deck without user approval.

## 1. Learner outcome alignment

- 1: The deck lists topics but does not state what the learner can do afterward.
- 2: An observable outcome exists, but sections or the recap drift away from it.
- 3: The goals, teaching sequence, practice, and recap support one observable
  capability.
- 4: The deck also makes the learner's before-state, success criteria, and
  transfer to a new situation explicit.

## 2. Conceptual progression

- 1: Slides form a feature tour, glossary, or disconnected list.
- 2: Sections have a plausible order, but prerequisites or transitions are weak.
- 3: Each section builds on prior knowledge and prepares the next decision or
  mechanism.
- 4: The sequence resolves a recurring problem in stages and uses earlier ideas
  in later reasoning.

## 3. Mechanism depth

- 1: The deck relies on labels, benefits, or broad claims.
- 2: Some mechanisms appear, but important state, interfaces, constraints, or
  failure paths remain unexplained.
- 3: Major claims name the relevant runtime path, data flow, state transition,
  API, policy, or decision mechanism.
- 4: The learner can trace cause and effect, including limits and observable
  failure signals.

## 4. Worked application and practice

- 1: The deck has no worked example or learner action.
- 2: Examples illustrate concepts but do not require reasoning or recur across
  the deck.
- 3: A practical anchor recurs, and at least two prompts ask the learner to
  predict, trace, diagnose, select, or critique.
- 4: Practice resembles the target real-world task, and the explanation reveals
  the reasoning, evidence, and likely mistakes.

## 5. Source and inference integrity

- 1: Important claims lack support or blur fact and inference.
- 2: Sources exist, but claim coverage, currency, or limitations are unclear.
- 3: Material claims trace to suitable primary sources, and the deck labels
  inferences, uncertainty, and version sensitivity.
- 4: Conflicting evidence, source limits, and consequential caveats shape the
  lesson rather than appearing only in references.

## 6. Visual teaching value

- 1: Visuals are absent where relationships are hard to follow, or they only
  decorate bullet lists.
- 2: Visuals support some concepts but omit important boundaries, sequences, or
  comparisons.
- 3: Major structures and flows have legible visuals that add information.
- 4: Visuals let the learner inspect, compare, or trace the mechanism and are
  reused when later reasoning depends on them.

## 7. Concision and distinctness

- 1: The deck is repetitive, padded, or dominated by interchangeable cards.
- 2: Most slides have a purpose, but several repeat ideas or contain too much
  text.
- 3: Each slide makes one useful teaching move, and the deck omits material that
  does not change learner understanding or action.
- 4: The deck has deliberate pacing and varied forms while preserving the
  shortest coherent learning path.

## 8. Transfer and operational usefulness

- 1: The learner receives no way to apply the material elsewhere.
- 2: Practical advice exists but remains generic or detached from failure
  signals and decisions.
- 3: The recap gives decision criteria, diagnostics, or a reusable procedure for
  a new situation.
- 4: The learner can explain when the guidance does not apply and choose a
  different approach using evidence from the deck.

## Review output

Report:

- the score and one concrete piece of evidence for each dimension
- the average score
- the two changes most likely to improve learning
- any factual, structural, or rendering findings from the normal validation
  workflow

Do not hide a serious issue inside the numeric average. State it directly.
